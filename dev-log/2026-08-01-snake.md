## Work Completed

### The three build blockers from the previous log are fixed

`Scene::Scene()` (`UE-0052`) now takes a real `SceneParams : SceneBaseParams` and
initializes its `entityManager`/`gameSystemManager`/`eventBus` references from it —
paired with a matching engine-side change: `Core::Scenes::SceneFactory` was changed from
`std::function<unique_ptr<IScene>()>` to `std::function<unique_ptr<IScene>(const
SceneBaseParams&)>`, and `SceneManager::ResolveScene` now builds a `SceneBaseParams` from
its own three member references and passes it to every registered factory
(`scene-manager.cpp`). This means the engine supplies those three dependencies to *every*
scene automatically — `GameplaySceneParams` just inherits `SceneParams` for the free
fields and adds `gameSettings`/`screenWidth`/`screenHeight` of its own, and
`SnakeApplication::Initialize()`'s registration lambda only ever names game-specific data,
never engine plumbing.

`EntityManager::OnRegistration()` (`UE-0053`) is implemented. `AppleSpawnSystem`,
`SnakeSpawnSystem`, and `SnakeGrowSystem` (`UE-0054`) were migrated off directly touching
`this->entityManager`/`this->eventBus` onto `GameSystem`'s public `AddEntity`/`RemoveEntity`
/`GetEventBus()` wrappers — those members are `private` on `GameSystem` on purpose, and the
three concrete systems were the last holdouts still reaching past that boundary.

### `Application`'s system registration was rebuilt around ownership, not return-type casting

`Application::RegisterSystem` returns the type-erased `Core::Systems::ISystem*` — casting
that back down to `IGameSystemManager*`/`IEntityManager*` isn't safe. `Application::Initialize()`
now constructs each system as a named `unique_ptr<Concrete>`, captures the concrete raw
pointer *before* moving it into `RegisterSystem`, and uses that captured pointer for
everything downstream (wiring `SceneManager`'s constructor args, assigning
`Application::sceneManager`, which changed from an owning `shared_ptr` to a non-owning
`Core::Scenes::ISceneManager*` since `SystemManager` is the real owner now). This also
fixed a bug where `Application::sceneManager` was never assigned at all — `GetSceneManager()`
would have returned a null reference the first time anything called it.

### `CMakeLists.txt`'s explicit source list was out of sync with the repo

Nine `.cpp` files that already existed on disk from the previous session's refactor were
simply never added to the `add_executable(...)` list: `engine/scenes/scene.cpp`,
`engine/systems/system.cpp`, `engine/systems/system-manager.cpp`,
`engine/systems/game-systems/game-system.cpp`,
`engine/systems/game-systems/game-system-manager.cpp`,
`snake-game/game-components/snake-body-component.cpp`, and all three game systems
(`apple-spawn-system.cpp`, `snake-growth-system.cpp`, `snake-spawn-system.cpp`). Once
added, a second wave of link errors surfaced (see below), followed by a small wave of
compile errors — `game-system.cpp` needed `#include "core/entities/i-entity.hpp"` (its
`unique_ptr<IEntity>` destructor needs the complete type, previously only forward-declared),
and `SnakeBodyComponent : Core::Components::IComponent` (`snake-body-component.hpp`) was
missing `public` on its base — private inheritance by default in a `class`, which made
`IEntity::GetComponent<SnakeBodyComponent>()`'s internal `static_cast` fail to see the
`IComponent` base.

### A cluster of "declared but never defined" link errors

Several classes declared a destructor or override in their header and never defined it in
the `.cpp`: `AppleSpawnSystem`, `SnakeSpawnSystem`, and `SnakeGrowSystem`'s destructors, plus
`Engine::Scenes::Scene::OnSceneExit()` and `SnakeGame::GameplayScene::Render()`. Since GCC
picks a class's first non-inline virtual as its "key function" and only emits the vtable in
the translation unit that defines it, an undefined destructor doesn't just leave a dangling
symbol — it means the whole vtable never gets emitted, which is why these surfaced as
`undefined reference to vtable for X` rather than pointing straight at the missing
destructor. `SystemManager`/`GameSystemManager`'s own constructors, and
`GameSystemManager::OnDebugUpdate`/`OnDebugRender`/`OnRegistration`, had the same problem
from the previous session's refactor never being finished.

### A dangling reference to a temporary killed entity construction with "pure virtual method called"

`EntityParams`/`SnakeSegmentParams` store `transform` as a **reference member**
(`const Core::Spatial::ITransform2D&`). `SnakeSpawnSystem::CreateHead()` built a
`Transform2D` temporary directly inside `SnakeHeadParams{...}` on one statement, then used
the resulting `headParams` on the *next* statement — binding a temporary to a reference
*member* doesn't extend its lifetime the way binding to a reference *variable* does, so the
temporary was destroyed at the end of the first statement, and `Entity`'s constructor
(reading `params.transform.GetPosition()` etc.) was calling virtual methods through an
already-destroyed object. Confirmed by `gdb` backtrace landing in `__cxa_pure_virtual`
inside `Entity::Entity`. `AppleSpawnSystem` never hit this because its equivalent
construction happens as a single expression. Fixed by giving `CreateHead()` a named local
`Transform2D` that outlives both statements.

### A chain of runtime bugs between "it links" and "it's playable"

Diagnosed live via `gdb` (breakpoints, `dprintf`-style logging commands, and — once — a
`-DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Debug` reconfigure of the gitignored `build/` dir to get real symbols),
in the order they were hit:

- `SnakeSpawnSystem::head` (`SnakeHead*`) had no initializer — reading it in `!this->head`
  was undefined behavior, and on this run happened to look non-null, routing `Spawn()`
  into `Reset()`/`TeardownBody()` on a garbage pointer instead of `CreateHead()`.
- After fixing that: `SnakeBodyComponent` was read via `GetComponent<SnakeBodyComponent>()`
  in three places but never *added* to any entity — `SnakeHead::OnRegistration()` only
  added `InputComponent`. `Append()`/`GetSegments()`/`Clear()` were all being called
  through a null `this`.
- Entities were being spawned at `Size2D{1, 1}` (hardcoded, both in `AppleSpawnSystem` and
  `SnakeSpawnSystem::CreateHead`) instead of `settings.boxSize` (which already existed,
  default `20`, and was simply never referenced) — invisible on a 500×500 window.
- `SnakeSegment::SetDirection` called itself recursively with no base case
  (`this->SetDirection(this->direction)`) — unbounded recursion, stack overflow, crash on
  any keypress that set a non-zero direction. Compounded by `size` defaulting to `0`,
  which made the recursive branch's guard condition true on the very first call.
- `SnakeSegment`/`SnakeHead` never initialized `size`/`speed`/`direction` away from their
  bare defaults (the old, now-dead `Snake::Initialize()` used to do this) — the snake
  never moved on its own, and `SnakeHead::Update()` didn't even call `Move()`.
- `SnakeSpawnSystem::CreateBody()`'s per-segment position math had two bugs: the Y
  expression was `(y / height) * height` — an identity, so every segment landed on the
  *same* Y as the head — and the X expression multiplied an already-correct per-segment
  offset by `width` a second time, placing segments far off-screen. Net effect: one visible
  head, the rest of the body invisible off-canvas, reading as "just a single box."
- `SnakeHead::Update` called `this->Step()` directly, bypassing the timer-gated dispatch
  in `SnakeSegment::Update` entirely — the head moved a full grid cell on literally every
  frame once any direction was set, reading as "constantly moving."
- `SnakeSegment::Step()`'s actual position update was nested *inside* the
  "is there a queued direction" `if` block, instead of running unconditionally every tick —
  meant movement only happened on the same tick a new direction was queued.
- `SnakeSegment::BeginPlay()` seeded `direction = this->transform->GetPosition()` — a
  leftover from an earlier iteration, before grid-ticking existed. Confirmed live via
  `dprintf`-logged `Step()` calls showing every entity's `direction` exactly equal to its
  own spawn coordinates. Removed; `direction` now starts at the header default (a small/
  zero vector) and is only ever set to a proper unit vector via input.
- `moveInterval` was being derived as `size / speed` inside `BeginPlay()`, collapsing to a
  fixed 2 seconds regardless of tuning — far too slow. Settled on removing that derivation
  entirely and using a flat header default (`0.2f`, five steps/sec) instead of deriving it
  from unrelated fields.
- Movement input used `BindAxis("MoveHorizontal"/"MoveVertical", ...)`, which fires again
  on key-release with a neutral value — that neutral event was stomping
  `pendingDirection` back to `{0, 0}` before the (2-second, at the time) tick ever got to
  read it, making input feel almost entirely unresponsive. Replaced with four discrete,
  edge-triggered `Bind("Up"/"Down"/"Left"/"Right", ...)` actions (`ActionRouter::Bind`,
  driven by `InputActionPressedEvent`, which only fires on press) — no neutral event
  exists for a digital action, so there's nothing to stomp the queued direction.
- Up/Down were inverted relative to raylib's Y-down screen convention (`Up` was setting
  `direction.y = +1`). Swapped the two literal signs in `SnakeHead::BeginPlay()`'s `Bind`
  callbacks — isolated, one-line-each fix, nothing rendering- or physics-side needed to
  change.

### Housekeeping

The old inline comment `// RAID BOSS: The Scene manager needs to be refactored to fit the
ISystem spec` is gone from `scene-manager.cpp` — `SceneManager` already inherits
`Engine::Systems::System` now, so that concern is resolved as a side effect of this
session's other work, not something that needed its own pass.

## Lessons Learned

### Components can't tick in this engine today — and that's a real gap, not just a Snake problem

Checked directly: `Core::Components::IComponent` (`core/components/i-component.hpp`) is
just a virtual destructor. Nothing generically calls into a component per-frame — `Render`
and `Input` each solved this with their own one-off mechanism instead
(`RenderComponent2DManager` walks its own registered list and calls `Render()`;
`ActionRouter::Update()` polls bound axes), not a shared capability. This came up while
discussing why `SnakeHead`/`SnakeSegment` had to carry their own movement state directly —
there was no other way for that state to get ticked.

### GCC's "key function" rule turns a missing destructor definition into a missing vtable

A declared-but-undefined virtual method — usually the destructor, since it's often the
first non-inline virtual a class has — means the compiler never emits the vtable at all in
any translation unit, not just a normal undefined-symbol error at the one call site that
needed it. Worth remembering the next time a linker error says `undefined reference to
vtable for X` instead of naming a specific missing function.

### Binding a reference *member* to a temporary does not extend its lifetime

Only binding a reference *variable* to a temporary extends that temporary to the
reference's own scope. A reference member initialized from a temporary argument at
construction time is not covered by that rule — the temporary dies at the end of the full
expression that constructed the *containing object*, even though the reference member
keeps pointing at where it used to be. This is exactly what made `SnakeSpawnSystem::CreateHead()`
crash only when the `Transform2D` temporary and the `SnakeHeadParams` construction were
split across two statements, and not when (as in `AppleSpawnSystem`) they happened in one.

### The event bus already supports arbitrary event data — no plumbing changes needed

Checked `IEventBus::Subscribe<TEvent>`/`Publish<TEvent>` (`i-event-bus.hpp:19-37`): both are
fully generic over the concrete event type, and `Subscribe` already round-trips the full
`TEvent&` to the caller's typed callback. `GameStartedEvent`/`AppleCollectedEvent` are empty
structs today purely because nobody's put fields on one yet, not because of a bus
limitation. Directly relevant to the `MovementSteppedEvent` planned for next session.

### Grid-locked vs. smooth movement, and axis vs. discrete input, for a classic Snake

Discussed whether Snake should use continuous (`speed * deltaTime`) movement or the
traditional fixed-grid-cell stepping — landed on grid-locked, since it's both the genre
convention and what the codebase's `accumulatedDistance`/`size` scaffolding and grid-snapped
input math were already halfway built for. Separately, discussed continuous `Axis1D`
actions (reusable across gameplay and a hypothetical settings menu) versus discrete,
edge-triggered per-direction `Bind` actions — chose discrete for now, since `BindAxis`
firing again on release (with a neutral value) is exactly what was breaking responsiveness,
and discrete actions have no neutral event to stomp state with at all. Axis-based reuse
across menu/gameplay was noted as a reasonable idea to revisit if/when a settings page
actually needs shared bindings, as long as every consumer remembers to filter the neutral
value itself.

## Architectural Decisions

### `SceneManager` assembles and injects `SceneBaseParams` at scene-resolution time

Rather than have every game-specific `*SceneParams` struct manually thread
`entityManager`/`gameSystemManager`/`eventBus` through from the composition root,
`SceneFactory` was changed to take `const SceneBaseParams&`, and `SceneManager::ResolveScene`
builds that struct from its own members and passes it to whichever factory is registered.
Game code (`SnakeApplication`, `GameplaySceneParams`) never names engine plumbing at all —
it only ever supplies game-specific data, and inherits the base params struct for the rest.

### `Application::RegisterSystem` callers capture the concrete pointer before the move, rather than casting the return value

`RegisterSystem` returns the type-erased `ISystem*`, which can't be safely cast back to a
derived interface. Instead of adding a templated wrapper or an unsafe cast, call sites in
`Application::Initialize()` now construct each system as a named `unique_ptr<Concrete>`,
grab the raw concrete pointer before handing ownership to `RegisterSystem`, and use that
pointer for everything that needs the real type. Keeps `RegisterSystem` itself simple and
avoids ownership ambiguity — `Application` never holds the `unique_ptr` itself, it's a
transient local before ownership moves to `SystemManager`.

### Grid-locked movement over continuous, and discrete per-direction actions over shared axes

Covered above under Lessons Learned — recording here since both were deliberate choices
with a rejected alternative, not just observations. Grid-locked: chosen over continuous
`speed * deltaTime` movement to match genre convention and the existing tick-based
scaffolding. Discrete `Bind` actions: chosen over continuous `Axis1D` specifically because
axis release events were breaking input responsiveness, and there's no equivalent failure
mode for a discrete, edge-triggered action.

### Movement is becoming a generic, attachable `MovementComponent`, not a `GameSystem`

The biggest decision of the session, made *before* any of it was implemented — full
rationale, code sketches, and diagrams are in
`design-docs/2026-08-01_195220_movement-component-architecture.md`. Summary: a
`GameSystem` implies "one engine-owned instance"; movement is a per-character trait,
so it belongs on a component that can be attached to anything (the design's litmus test:
attaching it to `Apple` should make Apple grid-step and take input with zero
apple-specific code). This requires giving components a real `OnUpdate` hook — currently a
gap in the engine, not just a Snake-specific workaround — and keeping the snake-specific
"body follows head" logic entirely out of the generic component, reacting instead to a new
data-carrying `MovementSteppedEvent` published on the existing (and, per Lessons Learned,
already fully capable) event bus. A "should events also carry callable behavior" idea came
up and was deliberately deferred as a separate, parallel concept (a request/command
system) rather than folded into broadcast events — no concrete need for it yet.
